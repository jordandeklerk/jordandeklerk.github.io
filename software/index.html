ArviZ

ArviZ is a Python library for exploratory analysis of Bayesian models. I’m a member of the core development team, where I work on statistical computation and algorithmic design.

ArviZ is now developed in three repositories: arviz-stats, arviz-plots and arviz-base. The original arviz repository is the legacy one. The majority of my work is in arviz-stats, on PSIS-LOO cross-validation and model comparison.

I’m a co-author of the paper describing the library in the Journal of Open Source Software.

Repositories

  • arviz Legacy

    Exploratory analysis of Bayesian models with Python.

  • arviz-stats

    Statistical computation and diagnostics for ArviZ.

  • arviz-plots

    ArviZ modular plotting.

  • arviz-base

    Base ArviZ features and converters.

Highlights

  • Subsampled PSIS-LOO, which makes cross-validation practical on large datasets.
  • Moment matching, which repairs unreliable leave-one-out estimates without refitting the model.
  • Exact refits and K-fold cross-validation, for cases where importance sampling isn’t enough.
  • Predictive scores such as CRPS, and model comparison for subsampled results.
  • The project’s AI usage policy.

EABM Book

Exploratory Analysis of Bayesian Models is the online book that teaches the workflow behind ArviZ, covering MCMC diagnostics, predictive checks, model comparison and prior elicitation. I’m one of its three authors, with Osvaldo Martin and Oriol Abril-Pla.

Chapters I worked on

  • Model Comparison Contributor Chapter 7

    The book’s theory chapter. Its sections on high Pareto k values and on datasets too large for full LOO-CV hold the theory I wrote for moment matching and subsampling.

  • Model Comparison for Large Data Author Chapter 8

    Subsampled PSIS-LOO-CV in practice, with worked examples and automatic subsampling for PyMC and Bambi models.

  • Moment matching for improved PSIS-LOO-CV Author Chapter 9

    Moment matching in practice, worked through on the roaches example, with how to read its diagnostics and automatic moment matching for PyMC and Bambi models.

  • PSIS-LFO-CV In review

    A chapter on PSIS leave-future-out cross-validation, open as a pull request.

ModernDiD

ModernDiD is a Python library for difference-in-differences that I created and maintain. It is written for applied researchers, economists and data scientists who estimate the effects of policies and treatments.

A two-way fixed effects regression, long the default way to run DiD, can give misleading answers when treatment effects differ across groups or change over time. ModernDiD implements the estimators that recent research developed to avoid that problem, covering staggered adoption, triple differences, continuous and intertemporal treatments, extended TWFE and sensitivity analysis.

Estimation runs in parallel on one machine, with GPU support for some estimators and Spark support for staggered DiD on panels too large for one machine.

mmmJAX Early development

mmmJAX is a Python library I’m developing for Bayesian marketing mix modeling in JAX, built for experienced practitioners. You write the model yourself as a Stan-style program of plain Python blocks, and the library makes none of the modeling decisions for you.

Everything around the model comes packaged, from data preparation and media transformations to sampling, budget optimization and plots.

mmmJAX is in early development. It is in alpha and not yet on PyPI, so for now it installs from GitHub.
